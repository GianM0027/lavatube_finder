# End-to-End Multimodal Detection of Lunar and Martian Lava Tubes

## Overview
The identification of lava tubes and subsurface cavities on the Moon and Mars is a strategic priority for the sustainability of future long-term planetary exploration. These natural structures provide shielding from cosmic radiation and micrometeoroid impacts, making them suitable candidates for human habitats and storage.

## Problem
Current detection methods rely on single-source orbital data, such as optical imagery or thermal radiance. These unimodal approaches often produce high false positives, confusing craters or shadows with cave entrances, and require labor-intensive manual validation.

## Approach
This work proposes an end-to-end multimodal framework that fuses heterogeneous data sources, including:
- Optical imagery  
- Thermal inertia maps  
- Laser altimetry  
- Gravitational anomalies  

The system learns cross-sensor correlations within a unified pipeline, improving the characterization of candidate subsurface structures.

## Goal
The objective is to reduce the need for manual validation and provide a scalable detection framework. The integration of diverse datasets aims to improve reliability and potentially surpass single-sensor methods.

## Impact
This research provides a technical foundation for the preliminary assessment of safe habitats, supporting future lunar and Martian exploration efforts.
